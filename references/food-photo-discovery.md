# Food, photo, and social discovery

Use this when dining, visual check-ins, social trends, or creator references materially affect the trip.

## Food preference mix

Ask the traveler to choose a preset or adjust the mix:

- Aesthetic dining: interior, plating, view, atmosphere, and camera suitability
- Highly rated value: strong repeat reviews and good price-to-quality ratio
- Local classics: representative dishes and established neighborhood favorites
- Adventurous or unusual: regional specialties, markets, unfamiliar ingredients, or distinctive formats

Also capture dietary constraints, reservation tolerance, queue tolerance, meal duration, and whether food is a primary trip goal. Do not infer aesthetic dining preferences from gender or age.

A preset may be expressed as a simple allocation, for example `40% aesthetic / 35% local / 20% value / 5% adventurous`. Use the mix to select meals across the whole trip; every restaurant does not need to satisfy every category.

## Photo and check-in profile

Ask which results matter most:

- Iconic landmark proof-of-visit
- Cinematic landscape or architecture
- Fashion, portrait, or lifestyle setting
- Street life and candid atmosphere
- Unusual, playful, or currently viral locations
- Low-crowd or less-copied compositions

Capture preferred visual style, willingness to wake early, tolerance for queues, camera equipment, mobility, and whether the traveler wants photos, short-form video, or a longer Vlog.

## Social search logic

Use the user's preferred platforms when accessible, including Xiaohongshu, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, local review platforms, and booking or map services. When there is no preference, combine a current social source, a broad review or map source, and official verification.

Build searches from:

- Destination and neighborhood
- Current year, season, holiday, or event
- Traveler intent such as `出片`, `机位`, `citywalk`, `氛围感`, `宝藏`, `避雷`, `排队`, `预约`, `日落`, `夜景`, or their current local-language equivalents
- Activity or cuisine
- Recency terms such as `最新`, `近期`, `新开`, or a recent month

Refresh vocabulary from current search suggestions, recurring titles, local creator language, and live trend pages when accessible. Treat slang as a discovery aid, not proof of quality.

## Engagement and quality signals

Consider likes, saves or favorites, comments, view count, recency, creator relevance, and repetition across independent posts. Weight saves and detailed comments strongly for practical travel intent. Normalize expectations across platforms and account size; raw counts are not directly comparable.

Inspect whether comments confirm the claimed view, food, access, wait, price, and current condition. Watch for copied posts, sponsorship, sudden coordinated promotion, old viral content, misleading camera angles, and locations that are famous online but weak in person.

Shortlist with a reason tied to the traveler, not popularity alone. For each selected place record the best time, exact viewpoint or dish, crowd strategy, reservation needs, filming rules, nearby itinerary fit, and a fallback.

Use official or direct sources to verify hours, access, prices, permits, closures, and filming restrictions before adding a social discovery to the executable plan.

## Placement

Group food and photo stops into the day's geography and energy curve. A beautiful restaurant across the city is a poor choice when it creates excessive transit. Use meals and photo stops as anchors, recovery periods, or transitions rather than a disconnected list.
