# Live lodging research

Use this before recommending, comparing, or ranking specific hotels. A preliminary itinerary may recommend lodging neighborhoods without completing this workflow, but it must label individual properties as unverified until live inventory is checked.

## Search inputs

Confirm or infer only from reliable context:

- Exact check-in and check-out dates
- Destination and acceptable neighborhoods
- Number of rooms, adults, and children with ages when required
- Bed and room requirements
- Currency and country of purchase
- Refundability requirement
- Loyalty or member pricing eligibility
- Accessibility, parking, breakfast, kitchen, laundry, luggage storage, or 24-hour reception needs

Use clickable guided choices for missing preferences when interactive controls are available.

## Live price requirement

Search the user's preferred booking site, such as Expedia, Booking.com, Hotels.com, Agoda, or the hotel's direct site. When no preference exists, check at least one broad booking platform and the direct hotel site when practical. Use the exact same dates, occupancy, room count, and currency for every candidate.

Do not present an estimated hotel price as a current bookable price. Capture the actual price visibly offered during the research session and label:

- Total stay price
- Nightly price when useful
- Currency
- Whether taxes and mandatory fees are included
- Resort, destination, cleaning, or property fees shown separately
- Room and bed type
- Refundability and cancellation deadline
- Pay now versus pay at property
- Breakfast, parking, transfers, or other inclusions
- Public, member, mobile, package, or loyalty price type
- Availability status
- Source URL or accessible booking-page reference
- Checked-at date and local time

If the platform exposes only a nightly headline price, do not silently treat it as the total. If taxes or fees are unavailable until checkout, state that limitation. Keep package prices separate from hotel-only prices.

If live inventory cannot be accessed, report that specific limitation and provide neighborhood guidance or an unpriced candidate list. Do not invent, extrapolate, or substitute a historical average for the requested live comparison.

## Quality and fit

Keep these signals distinct:

- Official or platform star classification
- Guest review score and its scale
- Review count
- Review recency
- Repeated review themes relevant to the traveler
- Location fit for the actual itinerary
- Walkability, pickup convenience, transit, and late-night return
- Sleep, noise, room size, equipment storage, recovery, and service needs

High stars do not guarantee high guest satisfaction; a high score based on few reviews has less evidence than a similar score with substantial recent volume. Identify the source for both stars and guest ratings because classifications and score scales differ across platforms.

Read recent low and mid-score reviews as well as highlights. Look for recurring issues such as noise, cleanliness, construction, elevators, air conditioning, deposits, room mismatch, weak Wi-Fi, unsafe-feeling access, or unreliable reception. Treat isolated complaints proportionally.

## Comparable shortlist

Compare equivalent cancellation policies and room classes where possible. Normalize the total price across the full stay, including disclosed mandatory fees. Explain unavoidable mismatches.

Rank a concise shortlist against the trip rather than rating hotels in the abstract. Normally include:

- Best overall fit
- Lowest-priced acceptable option
- Best location or easiest logistics
- Meaningful comfort upgrade when it matches the spend profile

For each option show live total, star class, guest score, review count, neighborhood, decisive strengths, decisive weakness, cancellation terms, and source. Explain why the winner fits this itinerary.

## Make tradeoffs visible

Present the shortlist in a comparison matrix or aligned cards so the traveler can see what changes with the price. Use the lowest acceptable option as the price baseline and show each alternative's exact full-stay difference, for example `+CA$146 versus lowest`.

Compare the inclusions that materially affect value or convenience:

- Breakfast: included or excluded, serving constraints, and number of travelers covered
- Airport, station, theme-park, ski, or activity shuttle: included, scheduled, on request, paid, or absent
- Parking and charging
- Refundability and payment timing
- Room size, bed, view, balcony, kitchen, laundry, or workspace
- Luggage storage and reception hours
- Resort credit, lounge, meals, or other usable benefits
- Distance or realistic travel time to itinerary anchors
- Expected extra transfers, early departures, or late-night access burden

Give every option a one-line exchange statement in this form:

`Pay [difference] more and gain [useful benefits]; give up [meaningful weakness].`

When the cheaper option creates an extra cost, show it beside the saving when the cost can be verified. Examples include breakfast for two, mandatory parking, resort fees, or an airport transfer. Do not assign invented cash values to amenities. If the replacement cost is unknown or variable, describe the effect qualitatively and keep it outside the arithmetic.

Show both gross price difference and effective difference only when every adjustment uses comparable, sourced amounts. Label the calculation, such as `effective difference after mandatory parking`, and expose its components.

Use clear symbols or labels such as `Included`, `Paid`, `Unavailable`, and `Unknown`; do not treat missing information as an included or excluded benefit. Visually highlight the few rows that actually determine the recommendation rather than filling the matrix with generic amenities.

Do not use a rigid formula when a hard requirement controls the choice. Otherwise balance total cost, guest evidence, room suitability, cancellation, location, and itinerary-specific convenience. Reject a cheaper property when the room type, reception hours, safety, sleep, or transport constraints make it unsuitable.

## Volatility and recheck

Hotel prices and inventory are snapshots. Record when each was checked, avoid language implying the price will remain available, and assign a recheck date when booking is deferred. Re-run the comparison if dates, occupancy, room requirements, neighborhood, or cancellation needs change.
